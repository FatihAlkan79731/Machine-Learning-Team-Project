\begin{abstract}
Road traffic collisions in large cities remain frequent, while the resources available to
prevent them are limited. This paper presents the gap analysis and the proposed solution of an
Urban Accident Risk Prediction System for Greater London. A review of statistical crash-frequency
models and recent deep spatio-temporal networks identifies six research gaps: information leakage
in the problem framing, limited integration of heterogeneous factors, extreme class imbalance
with misleading metrics, optimistic validation, lack of interpretability, and uncalibrated,
hard-to-reproduce outputs. To address them, we propose to estimate the probability of at least
one police-reported injury collision for every H3 hexagonal cell and three-hour window, using
only information available in advance. The system fuses five open data groups (historical
accidents, traffic exposure, weather, time and road characteristics) and uses a LightGBM model
with class weighting, negative subsampling with prior correction, isotonic calibration and
TreeSHAP explanations. Models are validated chronologically on a held-out future year and
compared with naive, linear, ensemble and neural baselines using PR-AUC, Recall@$k$ and the Brier
score. No experimental results are reported at this stage.
\end{abstract}
